The problem with off-the-shelf

Most Teams Do Not Outgrow Their CRM — They Work Around It

The tell-tale signs are always the same: a shadow spreadsheet, a naming convention nobody documented, three fields that mean the same thing, and a weekly report someone rebuilds by hand. The tool did not fit the process, so the process quietly bent around the tool.

We start from your actual pipeline — the stages, the handoffs, the approvals, the exceptions — and build a platform around it. Data model first, then automation, then the reporting your leadership team has been assembling manually.

  • A data model that fits

    Entities, relationships and lifecycle stages that match your business, not a template built for someone else’s.

  • Workflow automation

    Assignment rules, follow-up triggers, approvals and escalations that run without anyone remembering to.

  • Reporting leadership trusts

    Pipeline, forecast, cohort and activity reporting built on one dataset instead of five exports.

  • Connected to everything else

    Two-way sync with email, calendars, billing, support and the marketing stack you already run.

How we work

From Messy Spreadsheets To One System

A migration path that keeps the business running while the new platform takes over.

  1. 01

    Process Mapping

    We sit with the people who use the system daily and document the real workflow — including the workarounds nobody admits to in a kick-off meeting.

  2. 02

    Data Model Design

    Entities, fields, relationships and permissions designed and reviewed before any interface work begins.

  3. 03

    Build & Automate

    Interface, business logic and automation delivered in short cycles, with a working environment your team can trial early.

  4. 04

    Migrate & Validate

    Historical data cleansed, deduplicated and imported, then reconciled against source records before cutover.

  5. 05

    Adopt & Improve

    Training, rollout support and a change queue for the refinements that only surface once real work runs through it.
